# 5. Requirements Traceability Matrix

## 5.1 Purpose

The Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM) provides a structured link between customer requirements, proposed solution components, documentation deliverables, and verification activities.

The RTM is intended to ensure that each identified requirement remains traceable throughout the proposed solution lifecycle.

The traceability approach helps identify missing requirements, supports validation activities, and provides a controlled reference for future changes.

## 5.4 Traceability Controls

The following controls will be applied to maintain requirements traceability:

- Each requirement will have a unique requirement identifier.
- Requirement identifiers will remain unchanged unless formally approved.
- Changes to requirements will be recorded through the project change process.
- New requirements will be assessed for impact on the proposed solution and documentation.
- Requirements removed from scope will retain their original identifiers for historical traceability.
- Verification activities will be linked to the applicable requirement.
- Supporting documentation will be reviewed when requirements change.

## 5.5 Change Impact Assessment

Changes to customer requirements may affect multiple areas of the proposed solution.

For each approved requirement change, the following areas should be assessed:

1. **Technical Solution** — Determine whether the system design or architecture is affected.
2. **Documentation** — Identify user, technical, administrative, or operational documents requiring updates.
3. **Deliverables** — Determine whether project deliverables or milestones are affected.
4. **Verification** — Determine whether existing verification activities remain valid.
5. **Schedule** — Assess potential impact on implementation activities and delivery dates.
6. **Resources** — Identify additional technical, documentation, or SME resources that may be required.
